# Taste

## Workflow

- Rejects patch files as a deliverable — "we do not accept patches, buggy implementation": wants the feature implemented directly and completely in the codebase, planned out first, with production quality rather than a quick stopgap. Confidence: 0.75
- For ambitious subsystems, expects a genuine research phase before design — study the existing implementations, gather external literature/prior art, and derive the architecture from those findings ("do a deep research … and then build a new engine based on that research", "highly researched engine") instead of improvising. Confidence: 0.55

## UX expectations

- Uses Blender as the reference model for editor UX and wants equivalent control over creative choices instead of automatic behavior — e.g. "I want to set the camera myself for preview too, just like blender have such options". Confidence: 0.55

## Problem solving

- Pushes back on compensating workarounds and prefers fixing the root cause structurally — e.g. when a work bay had to float to accommodate hanging parts, he asked to regroup the dependency order (attach those parts later) instead of lifting the bay. Confidence: 0.6
- Judges generated motion by physical plausibility, not just passing tests: reports parts interpenetrating mid-assembly as a defect and expects the motion to follow the real mechanical joint (e.g. lift the hips first, then insert them along the joint axis instead of dropping through an installed leg). Confidence: 0.5
