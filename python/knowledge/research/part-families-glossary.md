# A Practical Glossary of LEGO Part Families for Small Sculpted Figures

Small sculpted figures such as BrickHeadz are cube sculptures wearing a face. The standard figure is about 23 plates tall, with a 4×4-stud head built from twelve bricks, a face two plates thick, a torso seven plates tall, and legs four plates tall; the arms attach to a 2×2 area on each side of the torso ([Belle-Ve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs)). Brickset's anatomy article describes the same core as twelve 22885 1×2×1⅔ bricks with studs on one side mounted on a 4×4 plate, with eyes on a 1×4 tile that has two studs ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)). Those proportions are the reason the part families below matter: most of the visible character is made by attaching thin, shaped, or sideways-facing elements to a small rectangular core.

Part numbers below are design IDs shown by the cited source. LEGO often has several molds for one shape, so a number may appear with a letter suffix (for example 4085a–4085d); where that matters it is noted.

## Bricks with studs on the side (SNOT bricks)

**What they are.** These are ordinary bricks with one or more studs on a vertical face instead of only on top. Common sizes include [11211](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/11211) (1×2, studs on one side), [22885](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/22885) (1×2×1⅔, studs on one side), [87087](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/87087) (1×1, stud on one side), [30414](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/30414) (1×4, studs on one side), [32952](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/32952) (1×1×1⅔, studs on one side), [52107](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/52107) (1×2, studs on two sides), [26604](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/26604) (1×1, two studs on adjacent sides), [67329](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/67329) (1×2×1⅔, studs on three sides), and [4733](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4733) (1×1, studs on four sides). The last is nicknamed the "Travis brick" in BrickNerd's SNOT survey ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/snot-basics-geometry-techniques-and-pitfalls-3-18-2021)).

**What they do in a figure.** Side-stud bricks let a build change direction without brackets, so they are the workhorses for heads, helmets, hair volume, and side-mounted detail. Brickset's Hulk review notes that the head core is twelve 1×2×1⅔ bricks with studs on one side ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/27257)), while the Captain Phasma review describes a helmet built around a core "with studs facing in five directions" plus another layer of side-stud bricks for the angular shell ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/31615)). The 1×2×1⅔ size is five plates tall, which is why Brothers Brick refers to "5-plate tall bricks with side studs" when describing Lando's hair and torso ([The Brothers Brick](https://migration-stage.brothers-brick.com/2023/05/10/lego-star-wars-brickheadz-40623-battle-of-endor-heroes-review)).

## The headlight brick

**What it is.** [4070](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4070) is a 1×1 brick with a single stud on one side, set low on the part. It is thinner than a normal side-stud brick in its narrow dimension — two plates rather than two and a half — which makes it a useful "half-plate" problem solver ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/snot-basics-geometry-techniques-and-pitfalls-3-18-2021)).

**What it does.** Its offset stud creates recesses, insets, and reversals that a regular side-stud brick cannot. The Endor Heroes review uses headlight bricks to attach R2-D2's legs so they sit inset from the body ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/94929)). In a figure, the same trick is handy for eye sockets, belt lines, and panels that need to sit slightly below the surrounding surface.

## Brackets

**What they are.** A bracket is essentially a plate with a perpendicular extension carrying studs on its side. Common examples are [99781](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/99781) (1×2–1×2), [44728](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/44728) (2×2), [99207](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/99207) (1×2–2×2 inverted), [99780](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/99780) (1×2 inverted), [73825](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/73825) (2×1 inverted), and [93274](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40616-1) (1×2–2×4). In a regular bracket the top of the extension is flush with the top of the plate; in an inverted bracket the bottom edge is flush with the bottom ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2022/01/20/isnt-that-stud-supposed-to-be-on-top-no-its-snot-building-sideways-using-lego)).

**What they do.** Brackets attach larger SNOT subassemblies to a studs-up core and are the standard way to introduce a half-plate offset, because the vertical extension is half a plate thick ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2022/01/20/isnt-that-stud-supposed-to-be-on-top-no-its-snot-building-sideways-using-lego)). Merlin's review of the Luke Skywalker BrickHeadz says the torso is structurally based on two 1×2–2×4 brackets ([Merlin's Bricks](https://www.merlinsbricks.com/article/lego-star-wars-40795-luke-skywalker-review)), and Belle-Ve's guide uses bracket arms so a shirt cuff can be added below the sleeve ([Belle-Ve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs)).

## Plates with clips, bars, and handles

**What they are.** This is a broad family of small connectors: [4085c](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4085) (1×1 plate with vertical clip; several clip variants exist), [4081b](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4081b) (1×1 plate with ring), [48336](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/48336) (1×2 plate with handle on the side), [63965](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/63965) (6L bar with stop ring), [87994](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/87994) (3L bar), [15712](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=75317-1) (1×1 tile with open O-clip), [2921](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40436-1) (1×1 brick with bar handle), and [28917](https://www.newelementary.com/2020/01/lego-brickheadz-review-cat-wedding.html) (1×1 brick with handle).

**What they do.** A clip can hold any element that is one plate thick, and it is considered legal as long as the clip can return to its original shape; forcing it open is where "illegal" begins ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/illegal-snot-stressful-techniques-for-sideways-building-9-7-23)). That makes clips and bars ideal for hands, straps, accessory mounts, and small ears. Belle-Ve explicitly uses the clip as a hand or glove ([Belle-Ve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs)); the Lucky Cat review praises the ear connection made with a 1×1 brick with handle ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2020/01/lego-brickheadz-review-cat-wedding.html)); and the Mandalorian BrickHeadz inventory includes an open O-clip tile ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=75317-1)).

## Jumper plates

**What they are.** [15573](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/15573) is a 1×2 jumper plate and [87580](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/87580) is a 2×2 jumper plate: a plate with one stud placed in the middle of a 2×2 stud area rather than over a normal stud position. The 1×2 version creates a half-stud offset in one direction; the 2×2 version does it in both directions at once ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2021/12/01/dont-underestimate-that-jumper-plate-half-stud-offsets-in-lego-models)).

**What they do.** Jumpers centre narrow details — a 1×1 nose, a single ear, a braid, a belt buckle — over a wider assembly. They also allow quarter-plate steps when stacked with other offset elements ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/lego-snot-advanced-techniques-and-implementations-5-12-21)). Rey's braid in the Rey & Kylo set is attached with jumper plates ([The Brothers Brick](https://migration-stage.brothers-brick.com/2017/12/12/lego-star-wars-brickheadz-41489-rey-kylo-ren-limited-edition-collectors-pack-review)).

## Tiles

**What they are.** Tiles are plates without top studs. The most useful small tiles for figures are [98138](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/98138) (1×1 round), its printed eye version 98138pb072 ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40728-1)), [25269](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/25269) (1×1 quarter-circle), [27925](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/27925) (2×2 round-corner "macaroni"), and [2412b](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/2412) (1×2 grille). Larger plain tiles — 3068 2×2, 87079 2×4, and 2431 1×4 — make the flat face and clothing panels; the standard BrickHeadz face uses a 1×4 tile with studs on the end as the eye plate ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).

**What they do.** Tiles provide smooth skin, clothing, and hair surfaces, and printed tiles carry eyes, mouths, insignia, and belt buckles. They also make smooth shoe tops and feet ([Belle-Ve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs)). The Hulk review describes printed 1×1 round tiles as the standard BrickHeadz eyes ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/27257)). Quarter-circle and macaroni tiles round off corners and fill curved edges; both appear in the BrickHeadz inventory for set 40728 ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40728-1)). Grille tiles read as vents or mechanical panelling and appear in the Mandalorian and Buzz Lightyear BrickHeadz ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=75317-1), [BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40552-1)); ToyPro describes the grille's use for vents and machinery accents ([ToyPro](https://www.toypro.com/en/product/1515/tile-1-x-2-grille-with-groove/orange)). A 1×1 round tile tucked under hair also works as an earring ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).

## Curved slopes

**What they are.** Curved slopes have a rounded profile instead of a straight incline. Useful sizes are [6091](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40728-1) (2×1×1⅓), [11477](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/11477) (2×1×⅔), [15068](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/15068) (2×2×⅔), [49307](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/49307) (1×1×⅔ double), [50950](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/50950) (3×1), [61678](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/61678) (4×1), [32803](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/32803) (2×2×⅔ inverted), [24201](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40436-1) (2×1×⅔ inverted), and [93606](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40728-1) (4×2).

**What they do.** Curved slopes are the main hair-making family: they create strands, bangs, beards, and the soft outer edge of a head. Brickset's Kylo Ren review says his hair uses 1×2, 1×3, and 1×4 curved slopes, accented with 1×1 slopes ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/33650)); the Luke Skywalker review calls out a 4×1×⅔ double curved slope on the top and rear of the head ([Merlin's Bricks](https://www.merlinsbricks.com/article/lego-star-wars-40795-luke-skywalker-review)); and the Black Widow review highlights 6091 and 11477 as new curved slopes in dark orange ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2017/02/review-lego-brickheadz-marvel-disney.html)). The same family makes capes, robe hems, and rounded clothing edges: Kylo's cape is built from curved slopes and tiles ([The Brothers Brick](https://migration-stage.brothers-brick.com/2017/12/12/lego-star-wars-brickheadz-41489-rey-kylo-ren-limited-edition-collectors-pack-review)), and Lady Liberty mixes smooth curved slopes with exposed studs for hair and dress texture ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/45601)).

## Cheese slopes

**What they are.** The cheese slope is [54200](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/54200), a 30° 1×1×⅔ slope. It is two plates tall, has no top stud, and has a half-plate lip at the bottom ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/basic-lego-techniques/)). The wider [85984](https://www.newelementary.com/2017/02/review-lego-brickheadz-marvel-disney.html) is a 1×2×⅔ "double cheese" or roof tile.

**What they do.** Cheese slopes are the cheapest way to add a small chamfer, a tooth, a lock of hair, or a grin. The Hulk uses black cheese slopes scattered over a 4×4 plate for hair and green double cheese slopes for ears ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/27257)); a custom Brick-o'-Lantern uses cheese slopes for the mouth ([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/10/31/build-your-very-own-brickheadz-brick-o-lantern-with-lego-instructions)); and the Go Brick Me review uses cheese slopes for beard construction and curved 1×4 slopes for a rounder beard ([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/04/29/exploring-personalization-with-lego-brickheadz-41597-go-brick-me-review/)). Because they are only two plates tall, several can be layered to suggest hair texture without adding a full brick of height.

## Wedge plates

**What they are.** Wedge plates have a triangular or angled footprint. Examples are [43722](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/43722) (72° 3×2 wedge plate, right), [51739](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/51739) (63° 2×4 double wedge plate), [29119](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40616-1) and [29120](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40616-1) (2×1×⅔ wedges, right and left), and the 2×3 pentagonal tile [22385](https://brickonaute.fr/gamora-lego-brickheadz-41607-review).

**What they do.** Wedges fill the diagonal gaps between a rectangular core and a sloped silhouette. They are especially useful for hair flicks, fringe, collar points, and robe edges. Brickset's Kylo review lists wedge plates among the hair parts ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/33650)), and the Endor Heroes review describes Luke's hair as a combination of tiles, slopes, and wedges ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/94929)). The Gamora review shows a pentagonal tile used as a flat, angled hair or clothing panel ([Brickonaute](https://brickonaute.fr/gamora-lego-brickheadz-41607-review)).

## Round plates and bricks

**What they are.** Round plates and bricks have a circular footprint. The basic sizes are [4073](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4073) (1×1 round plate), [4032](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4032) (2×2 round plate with axle hole), [3062b](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/3062b) (1×1 round brick), and [3941](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/3941) (2×2 round brick). The 2×2 round tile [14769](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40728-1) is a related smooth cap.

**What they do.** Round elements remove corners. They make cylindrical limbs, rounded joints, bases, and the backs of eyes, and they can act as a single-stud pivot or spacer. The 2×2 round brick appears in BrickHeadz set 40728 ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40728-1)), and 1×1 round plates appear in the Buzz Lightyear inventory ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40552-1)).

## Cones

**What they are.** Cones taper from a studded base to a point or small top. Common sizes are [4589](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4589) / [4589b](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4589b) (1×1), [3942](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/3942) (2×2×2), and [4742](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4742) (4×4×2).

**What they do.** Cones are for tapered points: hair spikes, horns, hat tips, finials, and tapered trim. ToyPro describes the 1×1 cone as a compact tapered piece for spikes and fine points that fits into tight spaces ([ToyPro](https://www.toypro.com/us/product/34879/cone-1-x-1-without-top-groove/green)). At BrickHeadz scale the 1×1 cone is usually the right size for a single lock of hair or a small horn; the 2×2 cone reads as a hat crown or a larger tapered mass.

## Domes and dishes

**What they are.** Domes are convex caps. [553](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/553) / [3262](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/3262) is a 2×2 dome, and [30151b](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/30151b) is a 2×2×1⅔ dome. Dishes are the thin, shallow relatives used for smooth round caps.

**What they do.** Domes and dishes cap a rounded head, helmet, or shoulder without the stepped look of stacked plates. The Endor Heroes review notes that R2-D2's angular BrickHeadz body gets its only real curvature from a 2×2 dish on top ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/94929)). In a figure, a dome is often the fastest way to make a bald head, a helmet crown, or a rounded pauldron.

## Hinge plates

**What they are.** [2429](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/2429) is a 1×4 hinge plate base and [2430](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/2430) is the matching 1×4 hinge plate top. Hinge bricks and click hinges are the sturdier modern relatives.

**What they do.** Hinges let a subassembly sit at an angle or fold through a range. Holger Matthes notes that the old "finger" hinge plates can even be twisted to make a 1×5 plate with studs on both sides, giving a 180° SNOT connection, although that shape is out of production ([Holger Matthes](https://www.holgermatthes.de/bricks/en/snot.php)). Modern hinges are not vertically centred, so they introduce their own offsets ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/lego-snot-hinge-techniques-and-element-design-11-28-22)). The Hulk uses a green hinge brick 1×2 base and 2×2 top plate to attach the arms ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/27257)) — a good example of hinges as posable shoulder joints.

## Technic pins in SNOT use

**What they are.** [2780](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/2780) is a friction pin, [4274](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/4274) is a half pin, and [3673](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/3673) is a smooth pin.

**What they do.** A half pin pushed into a Technic hole makes a protruding "stud" that can attach another element sideways; a Technic brick with a hole in line with its top stud is especially useful for this ([Holger Matthes](https://www.holgermatthes.de/bricks/en/snot.php)). Inserting a 4274 into a 3700 Technic 1×2 brick also produces a half-stud offset ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2021/12/01/dont-underestimate-that-jumper-plate-half-stud-offsets-in-lego-models)). In figures, pins and axles secure parts that must not spin: Kylo Ren's printed belt buckle is fixed with a Technic axle ([The Brothers Brick](https://migration-stage.brothers-brick.com/2017/12/12/lego-star-wars-brickheadz-41489-rey-kylo-ren-limited-edition-collectors-pack-review)), and 2780 appears in the Mandalorian BrickHeadz inventory ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=75317-1)).

## Minifigure accessories used as details

**What they are.** Minifigure-scale accessories are often exactly the right size to read as a prop, a hat, or a facial feature on a BrickHeadz. Examples include [3899](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/3899) (mug), [80179](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/80179) / [34173](https://brickarchitect.com/parts/80179) (spoons), [30162](https://www.newelementary.com/2021/06/jonas-kramm-number-eight-lego-parts.html) (binoculars), [35365](https://www.newelementary.com/2021/06/jonas-kramm-number-eight-lego-parts.html) (BrickHeadz round glasses), [4341](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40272-1) (cauldron), [4332](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40272-1) (broom), [90826](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40616-1) (broom bristles), [30031](https://www.bricklink.com/v2/catalog/catalogitem.page?P=30031) (handlebars), [4529](https://dashbrick.com.au/collections/minifigure-accessories) (saucepan), [4528](https://dashbrick.com.au/collections/minifigure-accessories) (frying pan), [61482](https://dashbrick.com.au/collections/minifigure-accessories) (handcuffs), and [30194](https://dashbrick.com.au/collections/minifigure-accessories) (scissors).

**What they do.** They add character quickly: a mug or spoon makes a hand pose read, glasses define a face, and a cauldron or broom turns a figure into a witch. The Witch BrickHeadz inventory includes a cauldron and a broom ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40272-1)); the Cho Chang inventory includes broom bristles ([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40616-1)); and Belle-Ve's Stan Lee MOC is finished with Captain America's shield and Mjolnir ([Belle-Ve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs)). The BrickHeadz-specific round glasses (35365) are a good reminder that some accessories are made for these figures rather than for minifigures ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2021/06/jonas-kramm-number-eight-lego-parts.html)).

## Capes, robes, and clothing edges

Capes are usually either a molded cloth piece or a brick-built shell. Captain Phasma's cape is swept over one shoulder and has a red streak along its edge, with the detail built rather than printed ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/31615)). Kylo Ren's cape is made from curved slopes and tiles ([The Brothers Brick](https://migration-stage.brothers-brick.com/2017/12/12/lego-star-wars-brickheadz-41489-rey-kylo-ren-limited-edition-collectors-pack-review)); Lando's cape is likewise a shaped cloth element ([The Brothers Brick](https://migration-stage.brothers-brick.com/2023/05/10/lego-star-wars-brickheadz-40623-battle-of-endor-heroes-review)). For robes and coats, the useful parts are curved slopes for the hem, wedge plates for diagonal edges, tiles for smooth panels, and brackets or side-stud bricks to step the clothing outward from the torso. Rey's robe uses angled plates fitted to 1×4 bricks with studs on the side ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/33911)), a compact SNOT clothing edge.

## Building terms

### SNOT (Studs Not On Top)

SNOT means "Studs Not On Top": any technique where the studs face sideways or downward instead of upward ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2022/01/20/isnt-that-stud-supposed-to-be-on-top-no-its-snot-building-sideways-using-lego)). It is also called sideways building. The main tools are side-stud bricks, brackets, headlight bricks, clips and bars, hinges, and Technic pins; the goal is to attach a shaped or tiled surface to a studs-up core without showing the underlying grid.

### Jumper offset

A jumper offset is a half-stud shift created by a jumper plate. A 1×2 jumper shifts a part half a stud in one direction; a 2×2 jumper shifts it half a stud in both directions ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2021/12/01/dont-underestimate-that-jumper-plate-half-stud-offsets-in-lego-models)). This lets a 1×1 detail sit centred between four studs, and stacking jumpers with other offset elements can produce quarter-plate steps ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/lego-snot-advanced-techniques-and-implementations-5-12-21)).

### Half-plate offset

A half-plate offset is a shift of half a plate thickness. It matters because brackets have a half-plate-thick side extension, while a side-stud brick is two and a half plates thick in its narrow dimension and a headlight brick is two plates thick ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/snot-basics-geometry-techniques-and-pitfalls-3-18-2021)). Combining these pieces produces the small steps that let a curved slope line up with a straight wall, or a tiled panel sit flush with a brick face ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/2022/01/20/isnt-that-stud-supposed-to-be-on-top-no-its-snot-building-sideways-using-lego)).

### Brick vs plate height and the stud grid

The basic vertical unit is the plate. A brick is three plates tall, a plate is one plate tall, and a stud is two and a half plates tall; the stud pitch is about 0.8 cm, and the gap between adjacent studs is 0.32 cm, which is exactly the thickness of a plate or tile ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/tag/half-plate-offset/), [Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/basic-lego-techniques/)). Those ratios are why a 4×4 head can be twelve bricks tall and why half-plate and quarter-plate offsets are meaningful rather than arbitrary. The stud grid also explains why a 1×1 plate can be wedged vertically between studs: the gap matches its thickness ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/basic-lego-techniques/)).

### Legal vs illegal techniques

"Legal" and "illegal" are fan terms for whether a connection follows LEGO's internal design rules. LEGO says its rules exist for stability and to avoid unnecessary stress on the pieces ([LEGO](https://www.lego.com/en-ca/service/help-topics/article/using-illegal-building-techniques-in-lego-ideas-challenges)). An illegal technique is one that stresses or deforms parts, has no proper connection, or is very hard to take apart ([Bricks McGee](https://www.bricksmcgee.com/glossary/illegal-build)). The classic example is the "pony-ear" trick — wedging a plate or tile vertically between studs — which was common in old official sets but is now classified as illegal ([Brick Builder's Handbook](https://brickbuildershandbook.com/basic-lego-techniques/)). Forcing a clip open, or using plates between studs in ways that compress them, are other examples ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/illegal-snot-stressful-techniques-for-sideways-building-9-7-23), [Game of Bricks](https://gameofbricks.eu/blogs/news/illegal-lego-building-techniques-to-beware-of-2020)).

The nuance is that there is no LEGO police; the rules mainly govern what official set designers may do, and MOC builders often choose "barely legal" connections deliberately ([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/barely-legal-building-lego-with-dubious-connections-7-2021), [Bricks McGee](https://www.bricksmcgee.com/glossary/legal/)). For a display figure, the practical test is whether the connection is stable, reversible, and free of obvious stress. For a model that will be played with or shipped, prefer the legal version.
