# How LEGO BrickHeadz Figures Are Built

BrickHeadz are a deliberately constrained system: a chunky cube head, a short
blocky torso, stubby legs, and a small display base. The official style guide is
consistent enough that fan builders can reproduce it, yet expressive enough that
hair, headgear and accessories carry most of a character's identity. This report
covers the standard skeleton, the face, the many hair and headwear techniques,
arms and accessories, and the order in which the model is assembled.

## The standard skeleton

The legs are four plates high and are usually built from four 1x2 plates
([Brickset, "The anatomy of a BrickHeadz"](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[Wikipedia, "Lego BrickHeadz"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_BrickHeadz)).
Designers vary this when the character calls for it: 41590 Iron Man uses
headlight/earring bricks in the legs, 41591 Black Widow uses 1x2 brackets, and
41595 Belle uses a different construction to suggest a dress at the same height
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Fan guidance likewise suggests turning 1x2 plates into shorts by using two tan
1x2 plates, or adding an extra SNOT layer plus slopes when a dress should
billow ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).

The torso sits on a 4x4 plate and is two bricks plus one plate tall
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Two `22885 Brick 1X2X1 2/3 W/4 Knobs` are used to mount the arms, and the front
and back bricks are often printed with costume details
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Brackets such as `99781 Angular Plate 1,5 Top 1X2 1/2` create extra front and
back fixing points for belts and similar details, as on Black Widow and Batman,
while 41596 Beast uses a 1x4 brick with four studs on its side to hang coat
tails ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Fan measurements put the torso at roughly seven plates tall, with a 2x2 side
area reserved for the arms ([Belleve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs/)).

The head is the signature element: twelve `22885` bricks arranged around a 4x4
plate, with 2x2 bricks and plates inside and a pink brick at the top that
represents the brain ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_BrickHeadz);
[Bricks for the Blind, 40674 Stitch instructions](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)).
That gives a head four studs wide and four studs deep, with a plate on each
side; the 4x4 plate at the bottom is often part of the torso design, and on
Belle it doubles as her neck ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Belleve Bricks describes the head as a 4x4-stud area on each side and the top,
with the face occupying about two plates of thickness
([Belleve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs/)).
An average figure is about 23 plates tall before hair or headgear is added
([Belleve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs/)).

Every figure stands on a small baseplate a little over 4 cm square and under
1 cm high ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_BrickHeadz)). The
black base typically carries a printed 2x4 tile with the BrickHeadz logo and
series number, and two studs that locate the figure
([Brickset, 41588 Joker review](https://brickset.com/article/26610/review-41588-the-joker)).
The Stitch instructions use a 6x6 black base for that model
([Bricks for the Blind](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)),
so the footprint can scale with the character.

## The face

The face is built as a separate slab that drops into the front of the head.
Printed 1x1 round tiles serve as the eyes and sit on the second row of studs
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
The standard eye element is catalogued as a 1x1 round tile with a BrickHeadz eye
print, with several design IDs covering different characters and colours
([Brick Owl](https://www.brickowl.com/catalog/lego-tile-1-x-1-round-with-brickheadz-eye-31468-102487)).
Those tiles are mounted on a 1x4 tile with two studs, which is itself fixed to a
4x4 plate; the surrounding face is tiled smooth
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
A common fan recipe is a 2x4 tile for the upper face, a plain 1x4 tile, a 1x4
tile with studs at one end, and two round tiles with two square tiles between
them for the eyes ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).

Mouths and noses are usually omitted altogether, which is one of the reasons
the theme reads as a caricature rather than a portrait
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/brickheadz-blueprints-a-study-of-shape-and-form-1-19-23)).
When a mouth is essential it is usually printed: the Joker's grin is printed on
a 1x4 tile ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26610/review-41588-the-joker)).
Beards can do the job sculpturally instead — on 40547 Obi-Wan Kenobi the beard
protrudes under the eyes and creates a pseudo-mouth
([Jay's Brick Blog](https://jaysbrickblog.com/reviews/review-lego-40547-obi-wan-kenobi-darth-vader-brickheadz/)).
Eye prints are not always the default black: Newt Scamander uses a dark-gray
eye variant ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2019/02/lego-review-brickheadz-2018-2hy.html)).

## Hair and headwear: the parts palette

Hair is where BrickHeadz builders spend most of their creativity, and a small
family of parts does most of the work. Curved slopes give round volume and are
layered in several directions
([Brickset, 41588 Joker](https://brickset.com/article/26610/review-41588-the-joker)).
Quarter-circle tiles (`25269`, `1/4 Circle Tile`) are used in quantity to define
hair and beards; 41597 Go Brick Me supplied eight of each in several colours
([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2018/05/lego-review-41597-go-brick-me-chris-mcveigh.html)).
`26601 Plate 2X2, Corner, 45 Deg` is another shaping part supplied in that set
for hair ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2018/05/lego-review-41597-go-brick-me-chris-mcveigh.html)).
Macaroni tiles — `27925 Tile 2x2 Curved Corner` — appear eight times in white in
the 40796 inventory, showing that the curved-corner tile is an official
BrickHeadz hair/beard shaping element
([BrickLink 40796 inventory](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40796-1)).
Cheese slopes (`54200`, `85984`) are likewise standard in these sets
([BrickLink 40796 inventory](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40796-1)).

Tiles are the finishing layer — hair is often tiled smooth on top and at the
back, while exposed studs are used deliberately for texture
([Brickset, 41588 Joker](https://brickset.com/article/26610/review-41588-the-joker)).
Brackets such as `99780` and `99781` turn the horizontal stud grid into vertical
attachment points ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2018/05/lego-review-41597-go-brick-me-chris-mcveigh.html);
[Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
`22885` itself is the workhorse SNOT brick for heads, helmets and ears, because
its four side studs face outward in a ring
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[Bricks for the Blind](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)).
Hinges extend the vocabulary further: the New York Comic Con version of Boba
Fett uses hinges for its cheekpieces
([Brickset, 41629 Boba Fett](https://brickset.com/article/38909/review-41629-boba-fett)).

## Hair and headwear: attachment methods

There are four main ways hair attaches. The simplest is straight down onto the
top studs of the head, which is why the top of the head is left as a stud field
in the base build ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
The second is side studs: `22885` bricks let a sub-assembly hang off the side of
the head, which is how Stitch's ears are fixed
([Bricks for the Blind](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)).
The third is brackets, which convert a top-facing stud into a front- or
back-facing one and let hair wrap around the brow or nape
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2018/05/lego-review-41597-go-brick-me-chris-mcveigh.html)).
The fourth is hinges, used where a panel must sit at an angle, as on the NYCC
Boba Fett ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/38909/review-41629-boba-fett)).
Complex helmets combine all of these: Captain Phasma's helmet is built around
the traditional core with studs facing in five directions, an extra SNOT layer
forming the angular visor, and two pairs of 1x2 slopes with lattice pieces over
the eyes
([Brickset, 41486 Captain Phasma](https://brickset.com/article/31615/review-41486-captain-phasma)).

## Common hairstyles and how they are made

**Short hair and the basic cap.** A boy's short hair is commonly two 2x4 tiles
over the top and back, with the top 2x4 built in hair colour above a skin-colour
2x4 at the bottom edge ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).
A mullet simply extends the hair a stud or two further down the back, and
"bumpy" hair swaps the smooth 2x4 tiles for 2x4 plates so the studs read as
texture ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).

**Fringe, bangs and side partings.** The head can be zoned for planning: the
top two rows are forehead, the third row is eye level, and the fourth is below
the eyes ([Belleve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs/)).
Hair that falls over the forehead is therefore built as tiles and slopes that
overhang the top of the face, and hair can be brought over an eye by letting
tiles hide part of the face ([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).
The Joker demonstrates the layered approach at its most extreme: his slicked,
pointy hair is built in five directions using layered curved slopes, exposed
studs, 1x1 slopes and ten green 1x1 quarter tiles
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26610/review-41588-the-joker)).

**Tall, spiky and messy hair.** Tall hair is built up gradually, and a wedge
plate can replace studs at the top to make the silhouette look messy
([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).
Spiky styles benefit from the same wedge and slope vocabulary; the 41597
instruction booklet includes a faux-hawk among its hairstyles
([The Brick Fan](https://www.thebrickfan.com/lego-brickheadz-go-brick-me-41597-review/)).
Superman's curly-Q hair shows how far a single unusual part can go: it is made
with an Unikitty tail ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_BrickHeadz)).

**Ponytails, pigtails and buns.** A ponytail is made by placing SNOT bricks
where the tail should leave the head and covering them with tiles
([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).
The Go Brick Me set includes ponytails and pigtails among its twelve hairstyles
([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/03/21/41597-1-go-brick-me/);
[The Brick Fan](https://www.thebrickfan.com/lego-brickheadz-go-brick-me-41597-review/)).
Princess Leia's double buns break the usual rule that headgear is just 1x2x1
bricks with exposed studs: each bun is four identical sub-assemblies, attached
so that it can rotate, and built using six `14719` corner tiles
([Brick Fanatics](https://www.brickfanatics.com/lego-brickheadz-star-wars-41628-princess-leia-organa-review);
[New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2019/02/lego-review-brickheadz-2018-2hy.html)).
A side ponytail is pulled tight against the head rather than hanging free
([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/04/29/exploring-personalization-with-lego-brickheadz-41597-go-brick-me-review/)).

**Long hair down the back.** For long hair, the hairpiece is moved down the
back by a few studs so it reads as length rather than a cap
([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).
Braids can be brought in front of the ears instead of behind them
([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/04/29/exploring-personalization-with-lego-brickheadz-41597-go-brick-me-review/)).
Newt Scamander's hair combines 2x4 plates with `6091` arches and `25269` quarter
tiles to get a soft, sweeping shape
([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2019/02/lego-review-brickheadz-2018-2hy.html)).
A bob is built so that it covers the ears and bumps outward as it descends
([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/04/29/exploring-personalization-with-lego-brickheadz-41597-go-brick-me-review/)).

**Hats, helmets and animal ears.** Caps, ribbons, headphones and glasses are all
part of the Go Brick Me accessory vocabulary
([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/03/21/41597-1-go-brick-me/);
[The Brick Fan](https://www.thebrickfan.com/lego-brickheadz-go-brick-me-41597-review/)).
Boba Fett's helmet relies on many small SNOT tricks to make vertical stripes and
fractional offsets, with a printed 1x4 tile visor, a black 2x2 tile with two
studs and 2x2 diagonal tiles
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/38909/review-41629-boba-fett)).
Captain Phasma finishes her helmet with tiles and curved slopes for a smooth
shell, and uses plate edges to narrow the mouth band
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/31615/review-41486-captain-phasma)).
Animal ears are usually side-studs sub-assemblies: Stitch's ears attach to the
sides of the head after the hair and back of the head are complete
([Bricks for the Blind](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)).
Ears are often omitted on human characters, and an earring can be implied by a
hairpiece that covers the ear with a 1x1 round tile underneath
([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz)).
Glasses are their own sub-genre: Go Brick Me introduced round and square
BrickHeadz glasses lattice pieces, each about 5.25 bricks wide and 2.25 bricks
tall with a single inverted stud underneath
([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2018/05/lego-review-41597-go-brick-me-chris-mcveigh.html)),
and a 1x1 plate behind them helps them sit correctly on the face
([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/04/29/exploring-personalization-with-lego-brickheadz-41597-go-brick-me-review/)).
Dumbledore in 41621 is a useful reference for glasses construction
([Belleve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs/)).

## Arms, hands, capes and accessories

The arm is a 2x2 plate with a `11476 Plate 1X2 W. 1 Horizontal Snap` forming the
hand ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Variants exist for body type: Beast uses `99206 Plate 2X2X2/3 W. 2. Hor. Knob`,
and the Hulk uses a muscular arm design
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
The 40796 inventory confirms two `11476` per figure
([BrickLink](https://www.bricklink.com/catalogItemInv.asp?S=40796-1)).

Capes and coats are usually slopes and angled tiles rather than cloth. Captain
Phasma's cape is built from curved slopes and angled tiles with stepped edges
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/31615/review-41486-captain-phasma)).
Belle's dress carries a hood on the back
([Brick Fanatics](https://www.brickfanatics.com/lego-brickheadz-star-wars-41628-princess-leia-organa-review)),
and Beast's coat tails hang from a side-stud brick
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz)).
Accessories are frequently brick-built: Leia's blaster is made from a black
lightsaber hilt ([Brick Fanatics](https://www.brickfanatics.com/lego-brickheadz-star-wars-41628-princess-leia-organa-review)),
Boba Fett gets a jetpack and an EE-3 rifle
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/38909/review-41629-boba-fett)), and the
theme has extended to wheelchairs and prams for characters who need them
([BrickNerd](https://bricknerd.com/home/brickheadz-blueprints-a-study-of-shape-and-form-1-19-23)).
The Go Brick Me set deliberately supplies hats, glasses and ribbons so builders
can personalize a figure ([The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/03/21/41597-1-go-brick-me/)).

## Build order

BrickHeadz are assembled from the ground up, but the head is a module rather than
a layer-by-layer continuation. The Stitch instructions show the sequence clearly:
lower body and legs, then torso, then the head shell, then the hair and back of
the head, then the ears on side studs, then the face and eyes, then the feet and
finally the baseplate
([Bricks for the Blind](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)).
The head shell itself is built as an open box of `22885` bricks around a 4x4
plate, with the pink "brain" brick at the top, before the face plate is inserted
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[Bricks for the Blind](https://bricksfortheblind.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/40674-Stitch.pdf)).
Hair sub-assemblies are commonly built separately and then dropped onto the top
or side studs, which is why unusual hairstyles can be swapped between figures
([Unnecessary News](https://unnecessarynews.substack.com/p/10-tips-to-make-custom-lego-brickheadz);
[The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/03/21/41597-1-go-brick-me/)).
The official 41597 booklet runs to 84 pages and treats each hairstyle and body
as a menu of sub-builds
([LEGO building instructions, 41597](https://www.lego.com/cdn/product-assets/product.bi.core.pdf/6234198.pdf);
[The Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/03/21/41597-1-go-brick-me/)).

## Why the system works

The fixed skeleton — 4x4 head, 12 `22885` shell bricks, 4x4 torso plate, four
plate legs — gives every figure the same silhouette, so recognition has to come
from colour, printing, and the hair/headgear sub-assembly
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/26620/the-anatomy-of-a-brickheadz);
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_BrickHeadz)).
That is also the practical lesson for fan builders: get the proportions and face
right, then treat hair as a separate model with its own attachment strategy
([Belleve Bricks](https://bellevebricks.com/designing-lego-brickheadz-mocs/);
[Brickset, 41597 review](https://brickset.com/article/35172/review-41597-go-brick-me)).
As the 41597 review notes, the hair makes or breaks the model
([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/35172/review-41597-go-brick-me)).
